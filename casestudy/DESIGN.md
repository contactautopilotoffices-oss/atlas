# Case study design bar

What "polished" means for the case study pages, written down so the template
can be judged and improved the same way every day. The bar is the deck the
Andheri team made by hand: a 33-page, 16:9 business case. This file
describes its design only. No figures from it belong here: this repository
is public.

## The page

- 16:9, 338.67 x 190.5 mm (PowerPoint widescreen). Screen and print are the
  same page.
- Every content page has the same frame: a thin running header (study title
  left, section right), one serif headline, the content, a source line and
  the page number at the foot. Nothing touches the frame's edges.
- One idea per page. A page that needs a second headline is two pages.
- Every page fills its height. A page whose content ends in the top half
  needs a larger chart, a bigger figure or a merge with its neighbour.

## Section openers

- Full-bleed photograph, darkened towards the bottom, the section number,
  title and one sentence in white, three big figures along the bottom.
- The caption "AI-generated illustration" sits in a corner, small, every time.
- Without a photograph, the band must still look deliberate, not empty.

## Figures and text

- Big numbers in the serif face, in the accent colour, each with a label of
  one short line under it and, where needed, a second line in a lighter tone.
- Every chart or table is paired with a "What it means for us" box: two or
  three consequences, each one sentence, on the right or below.
- Prose is plain spoken English. No em dashes. No marketing words.

## Charts

- Labels sit on or beside the thing they label and never overlap each other,
  the axis or another mark.
- Every axis says its unit once. Values are printed on the marks so the
  reader never estimates from gridlines.
- One accent colour for "ours", greys and muted blues for the market.
- A projection is visibly different (dashed or lighter), and the legend or
  label says so.

## Tables

- Numbers right-aligned in the mono face; text left. Totals ruled above.
- Group headers in small caps in the accent colour.
- A table that runs past the page is split, never shrunk below 7.5 pt.

## How the daily review scores a page

For each page, from its PNG:

1. Overlap: any text over text, a mark or the frame? (must be none)
2. Fill: how much of the page height is empty below the content? (under a
   quarter)
3. Hierarchy: is there one obvious headline and one obvious figure or chart?
4. Pairing: does every chart or table have its "what it means" box?
5. Type: anything under 7.5 pt, or a line longer than about 110 characters?
6. Consistency: header, footer, margins and colours the same as the
   neighbouring pages?

## Known issues

The daily review keeps this list current: it adds what it finds, removes
what it fixes, and fixes the worst one to three first.

- Several pages end in the top half (costing, levers, infrastructure, why
  here, the pipeline pages, risks, rivals next door): the content does not
  use the height.
- Recent deals: with no "what it means" copy the right third of the page is
  empty and the table has no pairing box.
- The terms table sets the small notes under big figures in the serif face.
- Price ladder: the dashed "Middle" line runs through the count label of the
  band it falls in, and the chart leaves a wide empty strip above the dots.
- "How full rival centres run": the band columns are narrow, so long
  "operator · building" lines wrap, and the page ends at about two thirds of
  its height. The bars could take the full width with the box below.
